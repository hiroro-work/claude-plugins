# Tier escalation

The one-way correction that raises a misjudged difficulty tier.

Unqualified `§ Tier criteria` / `§ Resolution procedure` / `§ Lanes` / `§ Difficulty-skip matrix` / `§ Row marking` references resolve to [`tier-assessment.md`](tier-assessment.md); unqualified `§ Configuration` / `§ Step N` references resolve to `SKILL.md`.

## Tier-change sites (closed list of 3)

Two are **escalation checkpoints** this file owns end to end — re-assess against § Tier criteria and act only when the new assessment is strictly higher than the current tier:

- **Step 2's Confirm difficulty sub-step**, once the plan body is drafted — before any code is written.
- **[`step5-implement.md`](step5-implement.md)'s "Tier escalation checkpoint" sub-step**, once every planned edit has landed — the point where an implementation that outgrew its plan surfaces.

The third is a **re-derivation site owned elsewhere**: [`step4-finalize-plan.md`](step4-finalize-plan.md) § Sub-step 3's express-lane re-activation, when a `rewrite-approach` material change lands on an express-lane plan. It re-runs § Resolution procedure in full rather than taking the incremental path below (the ledgers are rebuilt from scratch, not amended), **clamps the result upward to `max(pre-rewrite tier, new assessment)`** so this site cannot lower the tier either, and then applies this file's **Read what the express lane deferred** step when the clamped tier leaves the express lane.

## What an escalation does

In order:

1. Set the tier to the new assessment, reset `difficulty_skipped_steps = []` and `fast_mode_skipped_steps = []`, and re-run § Resolution procedure **steps 2–4 in full**. `plan_review_enabled` / `code_review_enabled` come out **re-derived from the run mode, config, and the new tier** rather than raised in place (one of the two exceptions to their monotonic-lowering invariant — [`step1-load-settings.md`](step1-load-settings.md) § Sub-step 4 — review-phase resolution), `subagent_model` is re-resolved, and both ledgers are rebuilt from scratch so no record keeps the old tier's token. This is the sole revision of § Configuration's "resolved once" reading: the value is resolved once per tier, and a tier change is what re-resolves it. **§ Resolution procedure step 5 (the difficulty log line) is excluded from the re-run**; step 4 below emits the superseding note.
2. **Reverse the row marking, forward of the checkpoint only**: for every row that was `completed` as a skip before this escalation and that **step 1's re-run left unmarked** — set it back to `pending`, provided **its step has not yet been passed**. **Review-phase rows carry one extra condition**: reverse `Step 3: Plan Review`, `Step 8: Code Review`, or `Step 8-1` only when that phase's re-derived flag (`plan_review_enabled` / `code_review_enabled`) is `true`. The unmarked test alone does not discriminate here: a phase turned off at Step 1 — the plan phase under `run_mode == "fast"`, or the code phase under a configured `code_review: false` — was registered `completed` back there, and step 1's re-run has nothing to mark for it at any tier (§ Resolution procedure step 2) — reversing on the unmarked test alone would reopen a row its own step then refuses to run (its Disabled-phase exception), leaving Step 4 sub-step 1 looping on it. **Source of truth**: [`step4-finalize-plan.md`](step4-finalize-plan.md) § Sub-step 3 — rewrite-approach bucket runtime's express-lane re-activation row-clearing condition; keep this condition in sync with it. Membership: the two review-phase flags and the three rows they gate, and nothing else — the quality-step rows that site also clears are not review phases and carry no flag condition. At the Step 5 checkpoint `Step 3: Plan Review` sits behind the run, so it stays `completed` — the plan is instead brought up to shape by step 3 below. `Step 8: Code Review` and its `Step 8-1` row sit forward of both checkpoints, so they return to `pending` **when the pre-escalation tier was Trivial** — the only tier that marks them at all (§ Row marking). Escalating out of Simple leaves them alone.
3. **Read what the express lane deferred**: when leaving the express lane, read [`simplicity-self-audit.md`](simplicity-self-audit.md) and re-run the full audit against the plan, and read [`plan-authoring.md`](plan-authoring.md) and restructure the plan to its § Template **document shape** — the required section set, their order, and the Build order step form (an escalation at the Step 5 checkpoint does this too). Its § Step 2 self-check is a different pass: the author's **content** checklist (does each Decisions item pass (a)+(b), is a judgment call buried in Build order), and it re-runs **only at the Step 2 checkpoint**.
4. Emit a one-line note in the resolved `language` naming the old tier, the new tier, the checkpoint that raised it, and the re-derived review phases. This note supersedes the standing difficulty log line. Take the review-phase clause's wording from § Difficulty log line's forms — but **name a phase there only when the resume point (step 5) still reaches it**: at the Step 5 checkpoint step 2 leaves `Step 3: Plan Review` `completed`, so name Step 8 (Code Review) alone. Paired bilingual samples (runtime rendering demonstration, both from the Step 2 checkpoint):
   - `language: ja`: `難易度を Simple から Moderate へ引き上げました（Step 2 の Confirm difficulty）。Step 3（Plan Review）を rules-only スコープで実施し、Step 8（Code Review）も実施します — Step 6（Tidy）/ Step 6.5（Polish Prose）/ Step 7.5（Rules Compliance Review）/ Step 11（Update Rules）を実施対象に戻します`
   - `language: en`: `Difficulty raised from Simple to Moderate (Step 2's Confirm difficulty). Step 3 (Plan Review) will run at rules-only scope, and Step 8 (Code Review) will run — Step 6 (Tidy) / Step 6.5 (Polish Prose) / Step 7.5 (Rules Compliance Review) / Step 11 (Update Rules) return to the run`
   In the **return to the run** clause, name whatever step 2 actually reopened: out of Trivial that list also carries Step 3 (Plan Review) and Step 8 (Code Review), and under `run_mode == "fast"` it carries neither Step 3 nor Step 6.5.
5. Resume from the point the checkpoint sits at. An escalation at Step 2's Confirm difficulty sub-step continues into **Step 3 (Plan Review)** when the re-derived `plan_review_enabled` is `true`, and into **Step 4 (Finalize Plan)** when it is `false` — Step 3's row stays `completed` and its Disabled-phase exception owns that skip. One at [`step5-implement.md`](step5-implement.md)'s Tier escalation checkpoint continues into **Step 6 (Tidy)**.

**A row already `completed` because its step ran is never reopened** — escalation returns skipped rows to the run, not finished ones.
